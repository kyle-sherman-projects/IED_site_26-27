Sketch Constraints Reference

A sketch that isn't fully constrained can move, stretch, or break when you change a dimension. Use this guide while working in Fusion 360.

Blue — Under-constrained
Geometry can still move. Missing constraints or dimensions. Do not extrude yet.
Black — Fully constrained
Every point is locked. Shape, size, and position are all defined. Ready to extrude.
Red — Over-constrained
Two constraints are in conflict. Fusion cannot solve it. Remove the conflicting one.

What they look like

Under-constrained

No origin point, no dimensions. The rectangle floats and can be dragged anywhere.

Fully constrained

Pinned to origin, dimensions applied. All lines are black — nothing can move.

Over-constrained

Two dimensions on the same feature conflict. Fusion highlights them red. Delete the duplicate.

Geometric Constraints (no dimension needed)

Horizontal
Forces a line to lie flat. Prevents it from tilting.
Vertical
Forces a line to be perfectly upright.
Coincident
Two points snap together. Use this to close gaps in a sketch profile.
Tangent
A curve meets a line without a corner. Used on arcs and fillets.
Symmetric
Mirrors geometry about a center line. Good for centered features.
Parallel
Two lines stay the same angle apart. Keeps sides from twisting.
Perpendicular
Two lines are at exactly 90°. The square symbol appears in the corner.
Equal
Two lines or curves become the same length. Good for squares.
Concentric
Two circles share the same center. Use on holes that must be aligned.
Fix (Ground)
Locks a point to its exact position in space. Use on one corner to anchor the whole sketch.

Dimensional Constraints (set a size or distance)

Linear Dimension
Sets the length of a line or the distance between two points.
Radius / Diameter
Sets the size of a circle or arc.
Angular
Sets the angle between two lines. Less common, but useful for angled features.

Constraining Strategy — Anchor First

Fix one corner to the origin
Right-click a point → Fix, or use a Coincident constraint between your corner and the origin point. This pins your sketch in space. Without this, the whole sketch floats even if all the lines are the right size.
Add geometric constraints before dimensions
Apply Horizontal, Vertical, Parallel, Perpendicular first. This reduces how many dimensions you need — a rectangle with Horizontal and Vertical constraints only needs width + height, not all four sides.
Add dimensions last
Use the Sketch Dimension tool (D key) to enter exact sizes. Each click on a blue line adds a constraint. Watch the lines turn black as you go.
Check: all lines should be black
When there are zero blue lines left, the sketch is fully constrained. Fusion also shows the "Fully Constrained" badge in the bottom status bar. If any blue remains, look for the floating line or point and apply the missing constraint.

Common Errors and Fixes

Symptom
Sketch still has blue lines even after adding dimensions to every edge.
Fix
The sketch isn't anchored to the origin. Fix one point or add a Coincident constraint between a corner and the origin. The shape is defined but its position in space is not.
Symptom
Fusion shows a red constraint conflict badge. Lines turn red.
Fix
Delete the last constraint you added — it conflicts with an existing one. Ctrl+Z to undo, or right-click the red dimension and delete it.
Symptom
Two lines look connected at a corner but the sketch won't close. Extrude won't work.
Fix
The endpoints only look coincident — they're not. Zoom in and apply a Coincident constraint between the two endpoints. A tiny gap breaks a closed profile.
Symptom
Changing one dimension makes the whole sketch distort in unexpected ways.
Fix
Add Horizontal and Vertical constraints to the sides first. Without them, lines can rotate when dimensions change. Constrain geometry before dimensions.
